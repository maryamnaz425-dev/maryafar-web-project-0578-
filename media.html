Hunza Valley: 3-Day Trip

Hunza Valley is a mountainous valley in the northern part of Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan. It is known for its breathtaking scenery, historical forts and warm-hearted people. A 3-day trip is the perfect way to experience the majestic peaks and serene lakes.

Best time to visit: Autumn (October-November) when the trees turn golden and red.

Day-by-Day Itinerary

  1. Day 1: Arrival in Hunza, visit Baltit Fort and Karimabad Bazaar.
  2. Day 2: Trip to Attabad Lake and Passu Cones. Enjoy local food.
  3. Day 3: Early morning visit to Eagle's Nest for sunrise, then departure.

What to Eat in Hunza

Hunza's food is simple, hearty and made for cold mountain air.

Chapshuro
A flatbread stuffed with spiced minced meat and baked until golden. Often called the signature dish of Hunza.
Diram Fitti
A sweet, dense wheat bread, traditionally served warm with butter or apricot oil.
Harissa
A slow-cooked dish of wheat and meat, stirred until soft and creamy. Perfect on a chilly evening.
Dried Apricots
Sun-dried apricots are a local specialty, and a great snack to take home.

Skardu: The Gateway to K2

Skardu is a city in Gilgit-Baltistan, known as the gateway to the mighty K2 and some of the world's highest peaks. It offers a perfect blend of adventure and peace, surrounded by cold deserts and turquoise lakes.

Highlight: Shangrila Resort (Lower Kachura Lake) and Deosai National Park.

Day-by-Day Itinerary

  1. Day 1: Arrival in Skardu, visit Shangrila Lake and Upper Kachura Lake.
  2. Day 2: Full day trip to Deosai National Park (Land of Giants).
  3. Day 3: Visit Shigar Fort and Cold Desert, then departure.

What to Eat in Skardu

Balti cuisine is rich, warm, and full of flavor.

Balti Curry
A signature spicy and tangy meat curry, slow-cooked in traditional Balti style.
Mamtu
Steamed dumplings filled with meat, similar to momos but served with spicy chutney.
Prapu
A traditional buckwheat noodle soup, perfect for the cold weather.
Apricot Soup
A sweet and sour soup made from local dried apricots.

Istanbul: Where Two Continents Meet

Istanbul is a major city in Turkey that straddles Europe and Asia across the Bosphorus Strait. It is famous for its rich history, stunning mosques, vibrant bazaars, and delicious cuisine.

Highlight: Hagia Sophia, Blue Mosque, and the Grand Bazaar.

Day-by-Day Itinerary

  1. Day 1: Explore the historic Sultanahmet area (Hagia Sophia, Blue Mosque).
  2. Day 2: Visit Topkapi Palace and take a Bosphorus Cruise in the evening.
  3. Day 3: Shop at the Grand Bazaar and Spice Bazaar, then depart.

What to Eat in Istanbul

Turkish food is famous worldwide for a reason.

Turkish Kebabs
Grilled meat skewers served with rice and fresh bread.
Baklava
A rich, sweet pastry made of layers of filo filled with nuts and syrup.
Turkish Delight (Lokum)
A family of confections based on a gel of starch and sugar, often flavored with rosewater.
Simit
A circular bread, typically encrusted with sesame seeds, sold on every street corner.
